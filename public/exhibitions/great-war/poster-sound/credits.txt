The posters' music (components/exhibition/RoomSound.tsx). Made mono, looped seamlessly (crossfade), levelled quiet.
poster1.mp3 (The Doughboys) — two Sousa marches, one after the other: "The Stars and Stripes Forever" (1896), then "In Memoriam" (1881), his funeral march for President Garfield. "Marching Music - The Stars and Stripes Forever By John Philip Sousa" (https://pixabay.com/music/marching-band-marching-music-the-stars-and-stripes-forever-by-john-philip-sousa-95379/) and "Marching Music - In Memoriam President Garfields Funeral - By Sousa" (https://pixabay.com/music/funerals-marching-music-in-memoriam-president-garfields-funeral-by-sousa-95349/), both by Nesrality (Pixabay Content License). Crossfaded.
poster2.mp3 (When the Guns Fell Silent) — two records, one after the other, both public domain in the US, from the Library of Congress National Jukebox (https://www.loc.gov/jukebox/) via the Internet Archive: Dvořák, Symphony no. 9 ("From the New World"): Largo, Victor Concert Orchestra, recorded 18 December 1912 (https://archive.org/details/loc-jukebox-132509-new-world-symphony-largo); then Handel, "Largo" (from Xerxes), Victor Sorlin, cello, recorded 23 October 1908 (https://archive.org/details/loc-jukebox-761374-largo-von-h-ndel). The records' own surface sound kept; rumble and hiss softened; crossfaded and looped.
floor.mp3 (and posters.mp3, the same) — the first 75 s of "The Stars and Stripes Forever", looped: heard from the doorway in the floor's hall (components/FloorSound.ts). The distant guns that were here before are now only in the silence room (../sounds/).
